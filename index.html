Section 01

Problem Statement

Can we predict whether a company is about to lose market share based on financial-news tone and market-performance signals?

Why early warning matters

Companies can face competitive threats before the consequences appear in financial statements. Our project investigates whether changes in financial-news sentiment and recent stock performance can provide an earlier warning signal.

BlackBerry illustrates the motivation: it held more than 50% of the U.S. smartphone market in 2007, yet its share fell below 3% by 2011 as competitive pressure intensified.

01

Detect threats early

Look for shifts in sentiment and market behavior before the damage appears in reported financial performance.

02

Measure predictive value

Test whether sentiment plus recent price history can predict next-day stock direction.

03

Support decisions

Explore an early-warning system that could help investors and managers identify competitive decline sooner.

Section 03

Methodologies

The workflow follows the same high-level structure as the AI4ALL GitHub Pages template: explain what was done, how the data was processed, and how the models were evaluated.

Data Ingestion

Financial-news headlines plus historical stock-price records.

Data Cleaning

Removed duplicates and nulls, aligned dates, and matched company tickers.

FinBERT

Converted headlines into positive, neutral, or negative sentiment features with confidence scores.

Feature Engineering

Added recent trading-day closing prices and other price-history features.

Modeling

Trained and evaluated Random Forest and Logistic Regression classifiers.

Preprocessing

We dropped missing rows, removed non-informative ticker-only headlines, filtered dates outside the matched stock-data range, normalized dates, matched news to stock records, and sorted news chronologically.

Sentiment Features

FinBERT transformed unstructured financial headlines into sentiment labels and confidence scores. FinBERT itself did not predict stock direction; its output became input features for the classifiers.

Random Forest

100 trees, maximum depth 10, balanced class weights, and 11 input variables. The same random state was used for reproducibility.

Logistic Regression

Standardized features, 1,000 maximum iterations, balanced class weights, and 11 input variables. This model was particularly useful for detecting decreases.

Deployment: The trained models were also placed in a Streamlit prototype that accepts a stock ticker, headline, and recent prices and returns a next-day stock-risk prediction.

Responsible Interpretation

Limitations

The model provides a useful proof of concept, but several factors limit how confidently its results can be generalized or used in real-world decisions.

02

Media visibility bias

Large or high-profile companies receive more news coverage than smaller firms. The model can therefore learn patterns that do not generalize evenly across companies or industries.

03

Changing market behavior

The matched dataset covers 2009–2018. Relationships between news, competition, and investor reactions may shift over time, so older patterns may not represent current markets.

04

Headline-only context

FinBERT was applied to headlines rather than complete articles, earnings calls, or filings. Headlines can omit context, nuance, and details that may affect sentiment interpretation.

05

Market-share proxy

The supervised target is next-day stock-price movement, not market share itself. Stock reactions are influenced by many factors, so a price decline should not be interpreted as direct evidence of competitive market-share loss.

06

Limited predictive strength

Accuracy was only modestly above baseline. The system is not reliable enough for autonomous trading or high-stakes financial decisions without substantially more validation.

Next Steps

Future Work

Future versions can strengthen the early-warning signal by improving the data, expanding the text sources, and evaluating models under more realistic conditions.

Use newer market data

Retrain and test on more recent periods to measure how well the approach survives market regime changes.

Add richer financial indicators

Incorporate technical indicators, fundamentals, sector context, volatility, trading volume, and other market signals.

Analyze deeper text sources

Move beyond headlines to full articles, earnings calls, SEC filings, and other documents that contain richer competitive context.

Test advanced models

Compare stronger NLP and multimodal approaches that can jointly reason over text and time-series features.

Focus by industry

Build industry-specific models so the system can learn competitive signals that differ across sectors.

Use chronological evaluation

Evaluate with forward-looking splits and rolling windows to reduce leakage and better simulate real deployment.

Improve calibration and alerting

Measure probability calibration and tune alert thresholds around the cost of false positives versus missed declines.

Expand the dashboard

Develop a real-time competitive-risk dashboard with explanations, uncertainty, and historical context rather than a single prediction.

Responsible AI

Ethical Considerations

Financial prediction systems can influence real decisions, so useful deployment requires transparency about uncertainty, bias, and the limits of automated forecasts.

A

Avoid overclaiming

Predictions should be presented as uncertain risk signals, not as facts or investment advice. The project’s modest accuracy makes clear communication especially important.

B

Guard against unequal coverage

News-volume differences can systematically favor large companies and underrepresent smaller firms. Performance should be checked across company size, industry, and time period.

C

Keep humans in the loop

Investors or managers should review the underlying evidence and business context before acting. Automated alerts should support—not replace—human judgment.

D

Explain model behavior

Users should be able to see which recent price and sentiment features contributed to an alert, along with model confidence and known failure modes.

E

Monitor model drift

Because financial markets evolve, a deployed system should be monitored for changing accuracy and recalibrated or retired when its assumptions no longer hold.

F

Prevent harmful use

The prototype should not be positioned as a tool for guaranteed returns or autonomous trading. Responsible use means emphasizing research, monitoring, and decision support.
